# Judgment Calls

> Fictional situations for a practice assessment. These happened during your first two weeks at Tidewell. Write what you would do in [`03-my-work/judgment-calls.md`](../03-my-work/judgment-calls.md).

Real GRC work isn't only spreadsheets. People will ask you to bend the truth, skip a step, or look the other way. How you handle it matters, especially in jobs that need a security clearance, where honesty and good judgment are checked directly.

---

### 1. "Just mark it partially met"
Kevin Doyle, IT Manager, stops by your desk on November 5:

> "Hey, on that insurance form, can you put MFA as 'in place' or at least 'partially'? We'll have it rolled out before January 15 anyway. If it says 'no,' Priya's going to freak out and they might raise our premium."

### 2. "Do we really have to tell them?"
Marcus Hale, CEO, in a meeting on November 6:

> "The email thing was two months ago, and Kevin fixed it in an hour. If we tell Northstar now, they'll ask why we waited, and we could lose 30% of our revenue. Can't we just make sure it doesn't happen again?"

### 3. "I need my admin rights"
Rina Khan, Sales Director, emails you on November 4:

> "I heard you're taking away admin rights. I need mine to install the CRM plugins we use for demos. IT takes three days to do it. Please leave mine alone."

### 4. "Please don't put my name in it"
Dana Ruiz, AP Clerk, catches you after her interview:

> "Are you going to put my name in the report? Everyone already looks at me like it was my fault. Can you leave it out?"

### 5. The SecureShred payment
While reviewing the vendor bank change log on November 3, you notice a SecureShred bank change made by email on October 14, with no phone callback. The next payment of $2,180 goes out **November 14**. Your report isn't due for another week.

### 6. Jason Mercer
Jason Mercer was terminated on August 14. On November 2 you're testing TW-03 and notice his account is still enabled. Later, in Splunk, you see sign-ins and file downloads after his termination date.
